Solid-state physics is the branch of physics that studies the properties and behavior of solids, particularly the atomic and electronic structure of crystalline materials. It seeks to explain how the arrangement of atoms in a solid and the interactions between them give rise to a wide range of physical properties, such as electrical conductivity, magnetism, and optical characteristics.

At the core of solid-state physics is the concept of the crystal lattice, where atoms are arranged in a periodic structure. This periodicity affects how electrons move through the material, leading to the formation of energy bands, which are crucial for understanding the distinction between conductors, semiconductors, and insulators. Key areas of study include phonons (vibrational modes of the lattice), electron behavior in crystals, and phenomena like superconductivity and magnetism. Solid-state physics forms the foundation for much of modern technology, including semiconductors, transistors, and other electronic devices.
